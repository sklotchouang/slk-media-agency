Most pages that answer this question for podcasters copy one benchmark table and leave out the month it came from. The numbers do move a little, so this article does the opposite. Every figure below comes from Buzzsprout's public stats page, read on 12 November 2026, with the month and the counting window named.

How many downloads is a good number for a podcast episode?

In October 2026, an episode with 28 or more downloads in its first 7 days was in the top half of episodes on Buzzsprout. That is the most recent month published when this was written.

The rest of the ladder climbs fast. 99 downloads in the first week put an episode in the top 25%. 425 put it in the top 10%. 1,034 reached the top 5%, and 4,658 the top 1%.

Buzzsprout explains why it uses the first week: "An episode's first week is the clearest sign of how it's doing, and a fairer measure than average downloads per episode." Average podcast downloads over a show's whole life favor old episodes that have had years to collect plays. A fixed 7-day window lets you compare a new show with an old one on equal terms.

How many downloads do most podcasts get?

Half of the episodes on Buzzsprout got 27 or fewer downloads in their first week of October 2026. That is the plain meaning of the median. If your episodes get 40, you are already ahead of most shows on that platform.

This is the single most useful fact for a new podcast. A small number of podcast listeners is normal in podcasting. It is not a sign the show has failed, and it is not what decides a successful podcast.

Keep one limit in mind. These numbers come from one hosting platform. Buzzsprout had 111,713 active shows in October 2026, which is a large sample, but shows hosted elsewhere are not in it. Treat it as a guide, not a census of every podcast.

Is 100 downloads per episode good?

Yes. In October 2026, 99 downloads in the first 7 days was enough for the top 25% of Buzzsprout episodes. 100 in a week means you beat three out of four episodes published that month.

Whether it is good for you depends on what the show is for. A show that sells a consulting service to a few hundred possible buyers can do very well with 100 of the right listeners. A comedy show chasing advertiser money needs far more. Decide what the show has to do first, then pick the number that matters for that job.

What counts as a podcast download?

A download is not the same as a listener, and Spotify's play is not the same as a download. Here is how each is counted, in the words of the organizations that define them.

Downloads from your host

The industry standard for counting downloads is the IAB Tech Lab Podcast Measurement Technical Guidelines. The current version, 2.3, was released in September 2026. Ask your host whether it follows them. Under those guidelines, a request counts as a valid download only when "the header information plus enough of the podcast content to play for 1 minute" has been downloaded.

The guidelines also filter repeats. A request is matched on IP address plus user agent (the app asking for the file), so the same app pulling the same episode again within the counting window, typically 24 hours, does not count twice. Requests from bots and data centers are filtered out.

A download still does not prove a listener heard it. An app can download an episode that is never played. That is why the number is a measure of reach, not attention.

Plays on Spotify

Spotify's analytics glossary defines plays as "the number of times any episode of your show was watched or listened to for at least 30 seconds on Spotify during the selected time period." That is a different count, from a different system. Spotify plays and your host's downloads will not match, and they are not meant to.

Spotify also lists a separate "Downloads" metric: "the number of times any episode of your show has been downloaded via your show's RSS feed on any podcast platform." Spotify says that metric is only available to shows hosted on Spotify for Creators.

How do podcast download numbers vary by platform?

Your podcast host counts downloads from every listening app that pulls your feed. Buzzsprout publishes which apps those downloads came from. In October 2026, Apple Podcasts made up 30.0% of Buzzsprout downloads, Spotify 24.9% and web browsers 18.2%.

Two practical points follow. First, if you look only at Spotify's dashboard, you miss most of your audience. Second, your own app mix will differ from these averages, and your host shows it. Check where your downloads come from before you decide where to promote the show.

How to find your own first-week number

  1. Open your podcast analytics on your hosting platform, not Spotify's. You want the number of podcast downloads across all apps.
  2. Read each of your last five episodes at day 7. Many hosts show downloads per episode over a date range. Set the range to the release day plus six days.
  3. Take the middle value. One viral episode or one flop will not skew it.
  4. Compare with the latest month on buzzsprout.com/stats. Use the same 7-day window. If your host uses another window, such as 30 days, do not compare it with a 7-day table.

Then do it again next month. Tracking your podcast's performance this way tells you far more than any single benchmark, because the trend of your own middle number is the metric that is yours.

How often should you look at your podcast downloads?

Once a week is enough. Check each new episode on day 7, write the number down, and leave the dashboard alone in between. Refreshing daily shows noise, such as a slow Tuesday, not progress.

Look at the longer view once a month. Ask one question: is the middle first-week number of my last five episodes going up, flat or down? If it is flat for three months, something about the show or its promotion needs to change. That is the point to run a proper audit.

How many podcast downloads do you need to make money?

There is no single number. No primary source we checked publishes a download threshold that guarantees income, and the ways to earn differ a lot. An advertiser will often ask for downloads per episode and for who the listeners are. A show that sells its own service or product needs buyers, not a big audience.

Acast, which sells podcast ads, gives its own view in the same article: "Sponsorships typically become viable around a few thousand downloads a month, but automated marketplace ads and listener support can start earning well below that." Treat that as one company's view, not a rule. A small show with a tightly defined audience can still sell to a sponsor that wants exactly those listeners.

Are podcasts still growing in 2026?

On Buzzsprout's own numbers, the platform got smaller over the last three years. It had 124,051 active shows and 140,256,454 monthly downloads in October 2023, and 111,713 active shows and 88,101,139 monthly downloads in October 2026. That is one host, so it does not describe the whole market. Buzzsprout says its totals cover every active podcast hosted on Buzzsprout, so shows on other hosts are not in them.

For your own show, the market trend matters less than your own first-week number. The top half line held at 27 to 32 the whole time.
